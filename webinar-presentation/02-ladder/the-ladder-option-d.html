The AI-Assisted Engineering Ladder — Option D

Framing: Hiring Einstein. The model is brilliant, but knows nothing about your company. Each rung is a step of his onboarding.

Version 1 · Five vignettes

You just hired Einstein. Now what?

Five steps to make a brilliant new graduate actually useful in your engineering company.

L0 · Chatbot
"Einstein in a room"
He sits in an office down the hall. You walk in, ask anything, get brilliant answers. Zero integration — he can't see your code, your data, or your simulations.
L1 · Agentic AI
"Laptop & credentials"
IT tickets filed. VPN, repo access, the simulation cluster, the ticket system. He can now do work — but every task starts from first principles.
L2 · + Skills
"Show him the ropes"
Two weeks shadowing senior engineers. He learns the standard simulation flow, the release checklist, what each internal script is for. Now he reaches for your playbook, not a generic one.
L3 · + Knowledge Base
"Open the archives"
He reads every post-mortem, customer report, design decision. He learns which rigs drift, which parts fail when, why approach X was abandoned in '23 — and contributes back to the archive.
L4 · AI Harness
"In the operating model"
On the on-call rotation. Scheduled responsibilities. Delegates to junior engineers. Audited. Reviewed. The company doesn't just employ him — it operates through him.
Einstein's IQ never changes. What changes is everything around him.
Version 2 · Onboarding journey

Hiring Einstein, one rung at a time.

Brilliant but green. Watch what changes as you fold him into the company.

L0 · Chatbot
Einstein in a room — useful, not integrated.
He sits down the hall. You walk in, ask anything, get brilliant answers. He can't see your code, your data, or your simulations. He goes home not knowing what your company does.
L1 · Agentic AI
Laptop & credentials — he can now do work.
VPN, repo access, the simulation cluster, the ticket system. He can read, run, commit, deploy. But every task starts from scratch — he re-derives your workflow each time.
L2 · + Skills
Show him the ropes — the company's verbs.
Two weeks shadowing senior engineers. He learns how we cut a release, how we triage a failed run, what each internal script is for. Procedures, encoded once, reused every day.
L3 · + Knowledge Base
Open the archives — the company's nouns.
Every post-mortem. Every customer report. Every design decision and why. Which rigs drift, which parts fail when, why approach X was abandoned. He reads it — and adds to it.
L4 · AI Harness
Part of the operating model.
On the on-call rotation. Scheduled responsibilities. Delegates to junior agents. Reviewed and audited. The company doesn't just employ Einstein — it operates through him.
Version 3 · Metaphor meets reality

What you do for Einstein — what it means technically.

Each rung is an onboarding step on the left, and the system you build on the right.

Einstein-side · onboarding moment
Tech-side · what you've actually built
L0
Einstein in a room.
Sits down the hall. You walk in, ask, get brilliant answers. Can't see anything you have.
Chatbot.
A conversation window. No file access, no tools, no actions. Useful for thinking out loud; copy-paste is the integration layer.
L1
Laptop, VPN, credentials.
He can read the repo, run the simulation, file a PR. He's an employee now — but a fresh one.
Agentic AI.
Tool use, file access, action loops. The agent operates inside your systems, iterating until the job is done.
L2
Show him the ropes.
Two weeks shadowing seniors. Learns how we cut a release, how we run a triage, what each internal script does.
Skills.
Reusable, on-demand procedures: the team's playbooks encoded so the agent stops re-deriving the workflow.
L3
Open the archives.
Reads every post-mortem, customer report, design decision — and contributes new findings back.
Knowledge base.
Retrieval over docs, incidents, customer context, decisions. Two-way: the agent both reads from and writes to it.
L4
Part of the operating model.
On-call rotation. Delegates to juniors. Audited and reviewed. Scheduled responsibilities.
AI Harness.
Hooks, scheduling, sub-agents, permissions, governance, observability. The system around the model is the product.
